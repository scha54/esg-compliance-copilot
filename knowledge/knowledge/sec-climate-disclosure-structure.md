# SEC Climate Disclosure Structure (Summary)

The SEC's climate-related disclosure framework requires registrants to disclose, where material: climate-related risks reasonably likely to have a material impact on the registrant's business, results of operations, or financial condition; the registrant's governance of climate-related risks; any climate-related targets or goals that are material to the business (including the scope of activities covered, the timeframe, and progress); and, for larger registrants, material Scope 1 and Scope 2 GHG emissions (Scope 3 disclosure obligations and thresholds vary by rule version and registrant status and should be treated as a scoping decision requiring confirmation for each filing).

Materiality governs what must be disclosed under SEC rules — unlike ESRS's structured datapoint list, materiality is assessed against the reasonable-investor standard for the registrant's specific business and risk profile. Disclosed GHG figures must be prepared using a consistent methodology, disclosed with enough context to be understood, and are subject to the same internal control expectations as other material financial disclosures. Any public climate claim referenced in or near an SEC filing (10-K, proxy, investor materials) is subject to the same anti-fraud materiality standard as financial statements.
